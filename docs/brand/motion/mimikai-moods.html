    The rings are toys

    More rings, harder questions

    The rings are not part of Mimikai's body: they are its toys. The class starts at level 1 with one ring, and every level up adds one more, so the juggle gets harder exactly when the questions do. Children can count the rings to see how far they've come.

    • One ring per level. Level 1 is a single ring passed from tentacle to tentacle. Each level up, Rayitas jumps through the new ring and it joins the juggle.
    • Streaks go past level 3. Cards only have three levels, so a team on a streak of 3 adds a ring in its own colour: mint for team A, coral for team B. Five at most.
    • The class's rings, the teams' rings. Level rings are sun, lilac and white, shared by everyone; only streak rings carry a team colour.
    • Same rhythm, higher throws. One throw every 0.45 s; more rings fly higher, like a real juggler.
    • Going down never fumbles. A level down or a broken streak sets the top ring down gently. No dropped rings, no failure animation.
    • Arms first. To wave, point, hug or high-five, Mimikai puts the rings down. At the end of class they fly into the energy ball.

    Mimikai's pet

    Rayitas, the tiger cub

    Mimikai has a pet of its own: Rayitas, a tiger cub who loves rings. Rayitas never talks and never comes out during a question. It shows up for the big moments: it jumps through each new ring when the class climbs a level, through every ring in the air when a team gets five in a row, and it chases a ring around the wall while the class waits.

    What Rayitas adds

    • A level children can see. "The tiger jumped through a new ring" is a level up a five-year-old notices, counts and retells at home.
    • A surprise, not a routine. Two to four visits a class at most, so children watch for it. A rare reward lands harder than a constant one.
    • One voice. Rayitas answers with its body; the praise and the words stay with Mimikai, so there is one character to listen to.
    • Baby proportions. Head as big as the body, short legs, the same ink eyes and pink cheeks as Mimikai: clearly its pet, clearly friendly.

    Guard rails

    • Friends, not a circus act. No whip, stool, cage or ringmaster hat, and Mimikai never orders Rayitas around. They play together.
    • No fire, ever. Children copy what they see. A hoop is something a child can safely jump through; a flaming one is not.
    • Never during a question. Only on the lobby, feedback and summary screens. In feedback the jump lands by 3 s, inside the 4 s screen.
    • Smaller than the hero. 35 shapes against Mimikai's 44, and about 60% of its height, so the octopus stays the logo.
    • Its own colour. Tiger orange #FF9A3D is only for Rayitas, never a ring or a team. The stripes use the brand Ink.

    End of class · after Duo's milestone

    The class energy ball

    Mimikai throws its rings into the air, where they fuse into a ball of light. Then every right answer the class gave flies back from its half of the wall into the ball, and it swells. The more the class got right, the bigger the ball and the bigger the ending. It plays once, just before the summary, and it belongs to the whole class: both teams fill the same ball.

    Why it works with children

    • They see their own answers. Each spark is one right answer, flying from the half of the wall where it was given. The ball's size is earned in front of them, not announced.
    • Everyone gets an ending. The smallest ball still glows, still pops, still earns a star. Effort always has a payoff.
    • Only earned stars appear. No empty star slots: a child sees one star won, not two missed.
    • A shared win before the score. In team games both teams feed one ball, so the class ends together before the summary names a winner.

    Guard rails

    • One soft flash, never strobing. A single 250 ms sun-coloured flash at 60% opacity. Projectors in dark rooms are bright, and some children are sensitive to flashing light.
    • Short. 4 to 9 seconds; the teacher's Next skips straight to the summary.
    • Once per class. A milestone stays special when it is rare.
    • Our own idea. Duo's clip inspired the build-up and release; the ball, sparks and rings are drawn from Mimikai's brand. Don't copy Duo's art or name the move after an anime.

    From Duolingo's owl to a classroom octopus

    What we borrow, what we change

    Duo is built to bring one adult back to an app every day. Mimikai plays to thirty children at once, in two teams, for one class. Same craft, different job.

    We borrow

    • A face for every outcome. The character is the feedback; text only confirms it.
    • Animator's basics. Anticipation before a jump, squash on landing, overshoot and settle. That is what makes a flat shape feel alive.
    • Rewards that grow. A right answer, then a streak, then a new level: each step looks bigger than the last.
    • Variety. Three lines per celebration, picked at random, so the twentieth "¡Muy bien!" still lands.
    • A personality. Curious, cheeky and warm. Mimikai copies the children (its name comes from mímica).

    We change

    • No guilt, no tears. Duo's crying and sulking pushes adults to keep a streak. In front of a class it would shame half the room. A wrong answer gets a nod and a heart.
    • Fair to both teams. The arm on team A's side of the wall cheers team A, the other arm team B. Nobody watches the pet celebrate against them.
    • Praise effort and strategy. "¡Pensaste rápido!" and "¡Buen intento!", never "¡Eres un genio!". Children who are praised for effort keep trying harder cards.
    • Never in the way. Small and slow while children choose; hidden during calibration.
    • Nothing after class. No reminders, no streak to protect at home. The TV keeps nothing.

    For the TV app

    Build notes

    One rig, cut from the logo

    The octopus on this page is the logo master split into layers: shadow, tentacles, left arm, right arm, head (body, shine, cheeks), face and effects. The rings sit on a layer of their own: up to five toys, never attached to the body.

    • Arms turn at the shoulders (76, 158) and (180, 158). A caught ring rests 20 units above a tentacle tip; between catches it follows its own throw.
    • Squash and stretch scale from the floor (128, 250); the head nods from the neck (128, 200).
    • Eleven faces swap in place: happy (the logo), open, think, grin, star, gentle, wow, sparkle, love, sleepy, wink.

    On Fire TV

    • Each layer is a static react-native-svg drawing inside an Animated.View. Only transform and opacity animate, with useNativeDriver: true, so the JS thread stays free for pointers.
    • No Lottie or Reanimated: no new dependency, the APK stays small and it all works offline.
    • A pure cueOf(moment, memory, now) in tv-shared/src/features/pet/domain picks the mood, so every rule below gets a unit test. The moods, rings, keyframes and art on this page are data in shared/pet, with the rig both apps draw Mimikai with.
    • Hidden during calibration: the phone reads the corner markers off the wall, and a moving shape on screen could be mistaken for one.
    • Every reaction peaks within 1.2 s and ends by 2.6 s, inside the 4 s feedback screen, so the answer's explanation stays readable.
    • The rings are one more layer: up to five circles moved by a single animation clock. The count is the class level (1 to 3), plus one for each team on a streak of 3 or more.
    • Rayitas is a second rig on the same clock: tail, four legs, body and head turn at fixed pivots (hips −22, −30; shoulders 20, −30; tail −34, −40). A hoop is drawn as two half-rings, one behind the tiger and one in front, so it really goes through. Built in shared/pet (tiger.ts, scenes.ts, rig/RayitasScene.tsx): the jump lasts 4.3 s in a 4.5 s level-up party, the big jump 4.3–5.8 s (more rings, more hoops) in a 6 s party.
    • The energy ball is built in shared/pet (energy.ts, rig/EnergyBall.tsx), every spark and particle a keyframe track on one clock. With the time Mimikai holds the last pose, the endings run about 6, 7, 8 and 9.5 s. A lesson's first class has no record to beat, so it gets the ending its stars earned.
    • Placement, like Duo: full screen over a dark wall for the class's greeting and for level-up and streak parties, with the moment's words under it; in the answer panel for each reaction; small at the bottom of the wall's centre line while children choose (between the two halves); big above the scores on the summary; centre stage in the lobby, where children can tickle it. One pet springs between the places, so its rings never stop.
    • A teacher setting, "Calm screen", shows each mood's still pose instead (also honoured on this page through reduced motion).
